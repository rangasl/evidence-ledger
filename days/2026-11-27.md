# Day 4 — Can single neurons predict your movement before you feel the urge? (Fried 2011)

**Track:** FW · **Claim ID:** FW03 · **Date:** 2026-11-27
**Verdict:** DEAD — single neurons were near chance. What survives (pooled activity just before the urge differs from activity 2 s earlier, in trials that all ended in a press) is predicted by every view of free will, so it is not evidence for the thesis.

## The finding
Fried, Mukamel & Kreiman (2011) recorded 1019 neurons in 12 patients with drug-resistant epilepsy.
The depth electrodes were already implanted for clinical reasons. There were 28 sessions of ~70 trials
each. Patients pressed a key with the right index finger whenever they chose. Afterwards they reported
where a Libet clock (one turn per 2568 ms) pointed when they first felt the urge (W). W averaged
**193 ± 261 ms** before the press. About 18–19% of trials were excluded (summed across reasons: 10% did
not wait a full clock turn, 5% W = press, 3% >20 s, <1% W >1500 ms before the press).
**17%** of medial frontal neurons (128/760) changed firing before W, against 8% in temporal lobe. In
SMA proper the figure was 23% (37/163). 55 neurons had already changed by 1000 ms before W. A support
vector machine (70/30 train/test split, permutation controls) separated pre-W windows from a baseline
window 2500–2100 ms before W. **Single units:** the best reached "almost 60%" at 500 ms before W, and
the average unit was near chance. **Pooled 256 SMA units:** >80% at 500 ms, ~70% at 980 ms. The
abstract's ">80% … 700 ms" does not appear in the results.

Two inconsistencies in the paper: the Fig. 6C caption says 150 SMA units, while the text says 256.
The Fig. 5 baseline is −2500 to −2100 ms, while the Methods baseline firing rate uses −2500 to −1500 ms.
The second is probably a different analysis.

## What it seems to show
This is cell-level data from inside the human brain, not scalp EEG (Day 1) or blood flow (Day 3).
Medial frontal cells, SMA most of all, change firing before people say they felt the urge. Some change
a full second early. Temporal-lobe units reached 70% only 80 ms before W, so the early signal is
regional rather than brain-wide. With 512 pooled units, accuracy was ~90% at 500 ms and >70% at
1000 ms. The decoder flagged an upcoming W in 98% of 3963 test trials, and its predicted time
averaged 152 ms before the reported W (SD 370 ms). Every test trial contained a W, so the 98% is
not a hit rate against moments when nothing followed. On this reading, the brain is already moving
toward the act while the person still reports no intention.

## The strongest attack
**The "single trials" were assembled from several patients.** The decoders used a pseudo-population
built "by considering units across all experimental sessions and subjects". The paper notes this
"assumes independence". So one decoded "trial" combines neurons recorded in different people on
different days. That is a statistical construct, not a reading of one brain. The only result from
cells recorded together is 37 units in one session: **73% at 500 ms**.

**There was no "no movement" comparison.** Every trial ended in a key press. The decoder compared
windows just before W with a window ~2 s earlier in the same trials. It never had to tell an upcoming
movement apart from a moment when nothing followed, so false alarms were not measured. The 98%
detection figure has the same limit. Schurger, Sitt & Dehaene (2012) showed for the scalp readiness potential that random
neural drift, lined up on the moment it crosses a threshold, produces a build-up without any early
decision. No one has simulated Fried's decoding curve this way, so this is a sufficient alternative
explanation, not a demonstrated reproduction. Fried's own interpretation is also threshold-crossing.

**A later study points the other way, with caveats.** Aflalo et al. (2022) recorded from posterior
parietal cortex in 2 tetraplegic participants. They say they replicate prior studies in general
terms. They argue the early activity begins "when the participant first chooses to perform the task",
and they "challenge traditional interpretations" that early activity fixes the behaviour. The caveats
are a different brain area and only two people, and it is not a replication of Fried 2011. Its
reading cuts against the "brain decides, awareness reads it out later" story.

**What was physically measured.** Changes in firing that predict *when* a single, meaningless finger
press comes, timed against a clock position recalled afterwards. Which-hand decoding was tried in only
3 patients, so the task tested *when*, not *what*. There was no veto condition. W timing is
self-reported, and the paper checks both sides: ±200 ms shifts in W fit the data, but shifts of
several hundred ms do not. The authors themselves write: "it remains unclear whether the emergence of
volition is causally related to the neuronal changes described." No direct replication or reanalysis
of Fried 2011 was found.

## Judgement
**DEAD.** The claim as worded died at once. Single units were at or near chance (the best reached
"almost 60%"), and ">80% … 700 ms" appears only in the abstract. The question is whether the
cut-down form still counts as evidence. It does not.

What was measured: firing aligned backwards from a recalled clock position, in trials that all ended
in a press, compared with firing ~2 s earlier (2.1–2.5 s in Fig. 5; Methods gives 1.5–2.5 s). That
can only show that activity changes as a press gets close. It cannot show that the press was settled
early: no-movement stretches were never sampled, and false alarms are unknown.

Every free-will position predicts this pattern. On the Schurger reading, noise lined up on its
threshold crossing builds up by construction (Fried's own reading is threshold-crossing too).
Compatibilism has no problem with unconscious neural causes. Libertarian views allow preparation
before a commitment made near W. A pattern every hypothesis predicts equally is evidence for none.

**Weaker form is evidence for the thesis:** no, because it is equally expected with or without free
will. At most it counts against a straw view that the felt urge is the first physical event, which no
one in the debate holds. The 37 cells recorded together (73% at 500 ms) have the same flaw.

What remains is a neuroscience fact that is not evidence for the thesis: medial-frontal neurons,
SMA most of all, change firing up to ~1 s before the reported urge. It is real and unreplicated.

**Would revive it (→ WOUNDED):** cells recorded together, decoded online, predict *whether* a
movement happens (against no-movement and veto stretches), at ≥75% accuracy, ≥500 ms before W, with
measured low false alarms. A no-decision accumulator fitted to the same data falls clearly short, and
at least one independent replication exists. Even then the claim reaches only "conscious initiation"
views, not compatibilism.
**Would revive it (→ SURVIVES):** all of the above, plus predicting *which* option is chosen in
choices that matter to the participant, ahead of a W timing shown to be reliable.
