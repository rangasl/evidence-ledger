Day 1 · Free will track
Does your brain decide before you do?

The most famous experiment against free will is Libet (1983). We gave it its best case and its strongest attack. 🧵

2/
Setup: people flicked their wrist whenever they felt like it and reported, using a clock, when they first felt the urge.
EEG showed a slow build-up, the "readiness potential", starting ~350 ms BEFORE the reported urge.

3/
The case for: the ordering has been seen again many times. Single neurons in 12 patients predicted the movement with >80% accuracy ~700 ms before people reported deciding.
So the felt "I decide now" is not the first event.

4/
The attack: that build-up may just be background noise. Average brain signals backwards from a movement and a ramp appears anyway (Schurger 2012).
It's no bigger before deciding to move than deciding not to, and people can still cancel after it starts.

5/
Worse: the "urge time" is reconstructed. Delay the feedback and people report deciding later.
And for choices with real stakes (donating $1000), the build-up was absent or much smaller (Maoz 2019).

6/
Verdict: WOUNDED.
What survives: for meaningless movements, brain activity starts before the reported urge.
What dies: "the brain decided unconsciously." It doesn't cover real choices.
