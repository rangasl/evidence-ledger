Day 5 · Free will track
Can electric current to the brain make you want to move, and does that mean "will" can be manufactured?

Desmurget et al. (2009, Science) stimulated the cortex of 7 awake surgery patients. Its best case, then its strongest attack.

2/
Right parietal cortex: a "strong intention and desire to move" a hand, arm or foot.
More current: patients said they had moved. The muscles never fired.
Premotor cortex: real movements, which patients denied making.

3/
The case for: the feeling of willing and the act come apart in both directions, and a stimulator can switch the feeling on.
So "I feel that I chose" can't prove a choice caused the action.

4/
The attack: a felt desire isn't an act of will. Nothing was weighed, chosen or done.
Every view of free will expects current in cortex to change experience. Also: a few patients, unblinded reports, no sham-controlled replication found.

5/
Verdict: DEAD.
Parietal current does evoke a felt wish to move. That shows how the feeling is made, not that free will is absent. Every position predicts it, so it isn't evidence either way.
