Day 2 · Simulation track
If one of three things must be true, are we living in a simulation?

The most famous argument for it is Bostrom's (2003). We gave it its best case and its strongest attack. 🧵

2/
The argument: simulating all human history needs ~10^33–10^36 operations. A planet-sized computer could do ~10^42 per second.
So either civilisations die young, or they don't run such simulations, or almost everyone like us is simulated.

3/
The case for: the arithmetic holds even if the estimates are off by several orders of magnitude.
You can't consistently believe all three: we'll reach that tech, we'd run many simulations, and we aren't in one.

4/
The attack: proving "one of three is true" is not evidence for the third. Bostrom himself splits his odds about evenly.
A Bayesian rebuild (Kipping 2020) puts the chance we're simulated just under 50%, with physical reality slightly favoured.

5/
It also undermines itself (Birch 2013): it trusts our physics to estimate future computers, but if we're simulated, that physics tells us nothing about the real one.

6/
Verdict: WOUNDED.
What survives: the trilemma, as logic.
What dies: "we're almost certainly simulated." Honest odds are about 1/3 to 1/2.
Would flip to SURVIVES: we build conscious simulations ourselves.
