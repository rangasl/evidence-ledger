Day 3 · Free will track
Can a brain scan predict your left/right choice 7–10 seconds before you know you've decided?

On Day 1, Libet's gap was ~350 ms. Soon et al. (2008) claimed seconds. We gave it its best case and its strongest attack.

2/
Setup: 12 people pressed left or right whenever they liked, then recalled which letter was on screen when they decided.
fMRI patterns in frontal pole and precuneus predicted the button at ~55–60% (chance is 50%), up to ~7 s before the reported decision.

3/
The case for: the signal sits in high-level areas, not motor cortex. Allowing for the slow blood-flow signal, the neural activity may start up to ~10 s early.
The same lab repeated it at 7T (Bode 2011, peak 57%). No independent replication yet.

4/
The attack: people choosing "at random" aren't random. Their previous choices alone predict the next one at ~62–64% (the lab's own data). The scan's ~60% is no better.
And 24 of 36 people screened weren't analysed, mostly for unbalanced choices.

5/
Verdict: WOUNDED.
In its defence, shifting labels by one trial found no trace of the previous choice.
But no one has shown the brain beats history in the same people. A small early lean, not a readout of the choice.
