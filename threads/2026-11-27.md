Day 4 · Free will track
Can single neurons predict your movement ~700 ms before you feel the urge, with high accuracy?

Fried, Mukamel & Kreiman (2011) recorded 1019 neurons in 12 epilepsy patients. Its best case, then its strongest attack.

2/
Setup: patients pressed a key when they chose and reported when they felt the urge.
Single neurons: the best reached ~60% at 500 ms before the urge. The average was near chance.
256 pooled SMA neurons: >80% at 500 ms, ~70% at 980 ms.

3/
The case for: 17% of medial frontal neurons changed firing before the urge, vs 8% in temporal lobe. 55 had changed a full second early.
With 512 pooled neurons, a decoder flagged an upcoming urge in 98% of test trials.

4/
The attack: each decoded "trial" pooled neurons from different patients and sessions. Only 37 cells were recorded together: 73% at 500 ms.
Every trial ended in a press. Random drift, lined up on the press, can produce this kind of build-up (Schurger 2012).

5/
Verdict: DEAD.
Single neurons were near chance, and "700 ms" is in the abstract only. What's left: pooled activity just before the urge differs from 2 s earlier. Every view of free will predicts that, so it's not evidence against it.
